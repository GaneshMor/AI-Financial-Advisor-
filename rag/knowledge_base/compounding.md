# The Power of Compounding

## What compounding means
With compounding, you earn returns on your original money (the principal) and also on the returns you earned earlier. SEBI describes it as earning "interest on the principal as well as previously earned interest". Each year the base grows, so the growth in later years is larger than in early years.

## Examples from the sources
- SEBI booklet: Rs 1,000 invested at 9% a year grows to about Rs 31,409 in 40 years with compounding, compared with Rs 4,600 with simple interest.
- NCFE booklet: Rs 100 at 10% a year grows to about Rs 260 in 10 years with compounding, compared with Rs 200 with simple interest.

## The Rule of 72
Divide 72 by the annual rate to estimate how many years it takes for money to double. At 6% a year, money doubles in about 72 / 6 = 12 years. It is an approximation.

## Why time matters
Because growth builds on earlier growth, the length of time money stays invested has a large effect on the final amount. Starting earlier gives compounding more years to work. The same principle works against you with debt, where unpaid interest also compounds.

## Limits
The examples above assume a steady rate every year. Real market-linked investments do not earn a fixed rate; returns can vary and can be negative in some years.

## How this app uses compounding (app note, not from the cited sources)
The app compounds monthly at the assumed annual return divided by 12. All projected values depend on that assumption and are illustrative.
