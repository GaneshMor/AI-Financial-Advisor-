# Gold as an Asset Class

## Gold in a portfolio
In SEBI's financial education booklet, gold appears as one of the asset classes in sample portfolios, typically 10% to 15% of the mix alongside equity and debt. It is used as a diversification component rather than the main holding.

## Ways to hold gold
- Physical gold such as coins or jewellery.
- Gold ETFs: Mutual Funds Sahi Hai explains that gold ETFs invest in high purity gold and "track the price movement of the physical metal more precisely". They trade on the exchange and are described as an alternative to owning physical gold.
- Gold funds: Mutual Funds Sahi Hai describes gold funds that invest in shares of companies involved in mining, processing, fabrication and distribution of gold. Their value then depends on those companies' share prices, not directly on the gold price.
- NCFE mentions gold ETFs and Sovereign Gold Bonds as alternatives to physical gold. Availability of specific government schemes can change, so check current official information.

## Risks (general note, not a quote from the cited sources)
Gold prices go up and down; gold can fall in value and does not pay regular interest like debt. Gold held through funds also has costs such as an expense ratio.

## App note (not from the cited sources)
The app keeps gold at 10% in the illustrative Conservative, Moderate and Aggressive tables and uses an illustrative 8% annual return assumption. This is not a forecast of gold prices.
