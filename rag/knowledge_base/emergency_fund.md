# Emergency Fund and Liquidity

## Why keep money for emergencies (general explanation)
Unexpected events such as job loss, illness or urgent repairs need money quickly. If there is no money set aside, a person may have to sell investments at a bad time or borrow at high interest. An emergency fund is money kept aside only for such events.

## Liquidity
SEBI's booklet describes three pillars of investment: safety, liquidity and return. Liquidity means how easily an investment can be turned into cash. It lists liquidity risk, the risk of not being able to convert an investment into cash quickly, as one of the risks investors face. NCFE similarly asks investors to consider return, risk and liquidity for every investment. Money for emergencies should be in safe, liquid places rather than in investments whose value can fall sharply.

## Emergency fund before long term investing
Building a basic emergency reserve protects long term investments from being broken early. Budgeting, which NCFE describes as comparing income and expenses regularly, helps find the money to build the reserve.

## How much (app assumption, not from the cited sources)
The cited SEBI and NCFE booklets do not state a fixed number of months. This app uses 6 months of living expenses plus EMIs as a prototype target and treats under 3 months as low. These are common rules of thumb used by the app, not regulatory requirements. People with dependents or irregular income may need more.
