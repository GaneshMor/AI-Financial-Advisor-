# Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)

## What a SIP is
A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a way of investing a fixed amount in a mutual fund scheme at regular intervals, for example once a month or once a quarter, instead of investing one lump sum. SEBI's financial education booklet describes it as a plan that "allows an investor to invest a fixed amount of money regularly in a mutual fund scheme". The minimum amount can be as low as Rs 500, and the money can be debited automatically through a bank standing instruction.

## How it works
Each instalment buys mutual fund units at that day's price (NAV). When prices are low, the same amount buys more units; when prices are high, it buys fewer units. This is called rupee cost averaging. SEBI's FAQ notes that a SIP lets investors "save and invest periodically over a longer period of time" and gives an opportunity to enter the market regularly.

## Benefits described by SEBI and AMFI
- Disciplined, regular investing that builds a savings habit.
- Small amounts are lighter on the wallet than one large investment.
- No need to time the market.
- Rupee cost averaging over market ups and downs.
- The power of compounding when returns are reinvested over a long period.

## What a SIP does not do
A SIP is a method of investing, not a product with a fixed return. The returns depend on the scheme the money goes into. Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks, and returns are not guaranteed.
