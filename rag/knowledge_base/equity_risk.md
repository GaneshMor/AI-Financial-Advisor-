# Equity Investment Risk

## What equity is
Equity means owning shares in companies, directly or through equity mutual funds. SEBI's FAQ describes equity or growth schemes as aiming for capital appreciation over the medium to long term, investing mainly in equities, with higher risk. SEBI's investor education page says such funds "have comparatively high risks".

## Why equity investments can lose value
- Market (systematic) risk: prices fall because of economy-wide factors that affect most shares together.
- Unsystematic risk: problems in a particular company or industry.
- Volatility risk: prices can swing sharply in short periods.
- Business risk: a company may perform badly or fail.

SEBI's FAQ lists market risk as the key risk affecting equity schemes. The value (NAV) of an equity fund changes every day with the prices of the shares it holds.

## Equity and time horizon
Because equity values can fall sharply in the short term, money needed soon has little time to recover from a fall. This is why equity is usually linked with longer time horizons. Even over long periods, returns are not guaranteed.

## Reducing, not removing, equity risk
Diversification across many companies and sectors reduces company-specific risk. It does not remove market risk.
