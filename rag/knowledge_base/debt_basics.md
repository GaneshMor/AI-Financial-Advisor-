# Debt Investment Basics

## What debt investments are
Debt investments lend money in return for interest. Examples include bonds, government securities, fixed deposits and debt mutual funds. SEBI describes debt instruments as having relatively low risk compared with equity, with returns coming as interest. Government bonds are considered effectively risk free from default because the government is not expected to default.

## Debt mutual funds
SEBI's FAQ describes debt or income schemes as aiming to provide regular and steady income by investing in bonds and other fixed income securities, with lower risk and limited potential for capital appreciation.

## Interest rate risk
The value of debt funds changes when interest rates change. SEBI states: "If the interest rates fall, NAVs of such funds are likely to increase in the short run and vice versa." So debt funds can also lose value, especially when rates rise.

## Credit risk
The borrower may fail to pay interest or repay the principal. SEBI's FAQ lists credit risk as a risk of debt instruments.

## Fixed deposits
NCFE explains that fixed deposits let you deposit money for a fixed period at a predetermined interest rate, with a penalty for early withdrawal.

## Role in a portfolio (general note)
Debt usually provides more stability and income than equity, which is why it is a larger share of conservative portfolios and short term goals.
