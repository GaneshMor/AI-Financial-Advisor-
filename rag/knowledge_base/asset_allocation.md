# Asset Allocation

## What asset allocation is
SEBI describes asset allocation as "a strategy for investing your money into various asset classes such as equity and debt". The mix should depend on the individual: income, risk appetite, goals and investment horizon. It is Step 3 of SEBI's five step financial planning process.

## Main asset classes
- Equity: shares or equity funds. Higher growth potential, higher ups and downs.
- Debt: bonds, fixed deposits, debt funds. Usually more stable, provides interest income.
- Gold: often held as a smaller part of a portfolio for diversification.
- Others such as real estate.

## Example allocations shown by SEBI
The SEBI booklet shows sample portfolios such as 60% equity / 25% debt / 15% gold, 70% equity / 15% debt / 15% gold, and 40% equity / 50% debt / 10% gold. These are examples of different mixes, not recommendations for any particular person.

## How risk and time horizon shape the mix
A person who can accept larger short term falls and has a long time horizon may hold more equity. A person who needs the money soon, or who cannot accept losses, may hold more debt. Spreading money across asset classes is also a way to benefit from diversification.

## Review
SEBI's planning steps include reviewing investments regularly and adjusting them as goals and circumstances change.

## How this app allocates (app note, not from the cited sources)
The app uses three illustrative tables: Conservative 30/60/10, Moderate 60/30/10 and Aggressive 75/15/10 (equity/debt/gold). Goals under 3 years use the Conservative table and goals of 3 to 5 years are capped at Moderate. Emergency fund goals use 100% debt. These thresholds and percentages are the app's own assumptions for education, not SEBI rules.
