# Mutual Fund Basics

## What a mutual fund is
SEBI's FAQ explains that a mutual fund pools money from investors by issuing units and invests it in securities according to the scheme's stated objective. Investors are called unitholders and share in profits or losses in proportion to their investment.

## NAV (Net Asset Value)
NAV is the unit price of a scheme. It is the scheme's net assets (assets minus liabilities) divided by the number of units outstanding. NAV changes daily as the prices of the securities change, and mutual funds must disclose it daily.

## Expense ratio
The expense ratio is the scheme's annual operating expenses expressed as a percentage of its daily net assets. For example, an expense ratio of 1% a year means about 1% of the scheme's assets goes toward running costs. The total expense ratio (TER) is disclosed to investors. Higher expenses reduce the investor's return.

## Main types of schemes
- Equity or growth schemes: aim for capital appreciation over the medium to long term; higher risk.
- Debt or income schemes: aim for regular and steady income from bonds and fixed income securities; lower risk; NAV affected by interest rate changes.
- Hybrid or balanced schemes: invest in both equity and debt, aiming for growth and income, usually less volatile than pure equity funds.
- Others include liquid funds, gilt funds, index funds, sectoral and thematic funds.

## Features listed by SEBI
Professional management, diversification, economies of scale, liquidity, and small minimum investments (SIPs from about Rs 500).

## Risks
Mutual funds carry risks, including market risk, interest rate risk, credit risk and liquidity risk. See risk_disclosures.md.
